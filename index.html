I joined a five-day fintech product management cohort to learn how lenders and no-code lending platforms work. Then I planned and built DealerFinance: a loan journey for new-car buyers at a dealership. I tested it live with about 15 to 16 people and fixed what broke.

My contribution

I defined the product, wrote the PRD and sprint plan, built the journey on the no-code platform (forms, journey and logic formulas), ran the testing and wrote up what I learned.

02

What I learned

Banks and lenders differ by what their money costs and which customers they choose. In a lending product the order of checks is a design choice: cheap checks first, costly ones later, and "no data" is a normal answer, not an error.

I wrote seven notes, and a table that links each class topic to where I used it in the product.

03

What I built

DealerFinance is a six-step journey. It collects consent by one-time code, verifies identity through DigiLocker, works out the price and loan, shows which lenders are likely to accept, sends the case to one lender, reroutes after a rejection and settles the dealer's trade advance and commission.

It runs on a no-code platform, for Kia cars and four lenders. Lender terms, rates and limits are invented test values.

04

What broke, and the fixes

At the live demo, customers with no credit score and customers with no car to exchange hit dead ends. I fixed both, and the second change also saved a paid lookup per case. My own testing found a loan formula that ignored the exchange value, which is still open.
